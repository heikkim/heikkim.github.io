I have seen this picture too many times. The board asks for an AI strategy, a consultant proposes a three-year roadmap, and the result is 40 slides that have nothing to do with the business.

The starting point is somewhere else.

## Start from the problem, not from the tool

A strategy that starts from a product is product strategy with a bad product. It is easy to sell, hard to deliver, and it usually ends before the first budget round.

The practical way of working is much simpler:

- Which problem do we solve first?
- What data does the solution need, and do we have it?
- Who owns the outcome, and how is it measured?
- How do we see that it worked?

Without answers to those, you do not have a strategy. You have a shopping list.

<!--content-image-->

## An outside view is a gift, not a product

A company always sees its own problem from too close. That is not a bad observation, it is just human. An outside look does not bring new technology, it brings a new way of naming the problem.

That is usually the cheapest part of the whole project.

Once the problem is named properly, the choices narrow by themselves: you do not need to buy everything, you do not need to build everything, and every solution can be measured.

Good AI consulting is usually short. Then you do the thing that matters, and you leave the rest undone.
