A CEO sits in two places at once: in decisions and in meetings. The first is supposed to make the second unnecessary, but more often AI programmes add more work to the same calendar than existed before.

What I keep seeing is that the bottleneck is never the model or the budget. It is the calendar of the person who has to say yes.

## Where the time really goes

When I map these programmes, the split is always the same. Most of the executive week went to:

- Status updates that could have been written.
- Meetings whose only output was another meeting.
- Things somebody else could have handled.

AI does not fix that problem. It just puts more tasks on the same calendar if you do nothing about it.

<!--content-image-->

## Delegation takes trust, not just a tool

The most I have seen companies gain is when the executive delegates their own work to AI and at the same time delegates their decision-making to another person. These are two different things, even though they are often treated as one.

Delegating work to AI is easy once the first prompt works. Delegating a decision to a person is hard, because it means saying out loud what your own judgement is based on.

If an executive cannot say that out loud, AI does not free their time. It only documents how much of their time goes into things that have nothing to do with AI at all.

## What I do myself

My own experience is that the best AI investment is not a model, it is an afternoon. It is time where you can work on the one thing that cannot be delegated.

When that is booked into the calendar on top of everything else, it does not stay. Then it is an investment that pays for itself.
