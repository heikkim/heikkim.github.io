## AI productivity & use cases

The AI productivity puzzle is yet to be solved.

*"Ninety-seven percent of organisations cannot reach their cost savings goals through productivity alone."* ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-26-gartner-hr-research-finds-77-percent-of-board-members-expect-ai-to-deliver-productivity-and-cost-savings-yet-just-3-percent-of-organizations-exceed-cost-savings-goals-via-productivity), October 2026). This is contrasted by strong belief in AI, as four out of every five board members *"expect AI will deliver productivity and cost savings."*

The story really short is that **AI has the potential to increase productivity**. The benefits of AI become tangible when the work being carried out actually changes for the better. It all starts with naming one place in your business where the change would matter, and then repeating this discovery. What you want in practice is a concrete list of AI use cases where value can be uncovered. 

A use case needs to be something specific. It also needs to be a piece of work that happens repeatedly, often enough to matter. It also has to have something relevant to do with an outcome a customer of yours is experiencing. Otherwise you should cross it off your list right now.

> Point a model at a process that was never questioned and you get the same process, **maybe** running faster. **Sometimes the right decision is to re-design!**

Don't forget to separate wishes from the real things that move the needle. "We should use AI more" is a wish, cross it. "The weekly customer report requires no manual work to assemble and the numbers come automatically from four systems" is a concrete business use case. 

Finding the candidates calls for discipline rather than inspiration. 

After you've uncovered the list, the most important next step is to name a leader who's accountable in delivering on it. That's already 50 % done in getting the project off the ground!

## Finding the candidates in five steps

Start with the work itself. Pick a process that runs repeatedly and has volume behind it, then walk it through from start to finish with the person who runs it.

1. **Pick a process with volume.** E.g. Customer onboarding, invoicing, offer quoting, weekly reporting. (Repetition.)
2. **Find out where it slows down.** Work handoffs, retyping information, waiting for decision, correcting somebody else's work. (Time & mistakes.)
3. **Test it against three areas.** Volume, checkability and cost. (Qualification.)
- Volume means it happens often enough across the company to matter. 
- Checkability means a person can tell difference whether an output is good or bad. 
- Cost means there is a price tag for doing it slowly or in a wrong way.
4. **Size it.** Multiply the frequency by the time each run takes. (Prioritization!)
5. **Slice it.** Twenty use cases is a wish list. Two to three with a designated owner for each is a plan. (Capacity!)

Cutting the list can seem like a bad idea, but it really is not. It is to ensure how the ones that survive get the attention they need. Plus, the leader accountable to deliver will be able to demonstrate quick wins to the people in the company!

## Engine losing rev

There are a few common failure modes that cause the initiative to stall and the company wasting time as a result. 

**Without clear ownership and a mandate to act**, the use case list effectively belongs to everybody, and to nobody at the same time. What follows is that one team suboptimises, that is they end up solving their own problems instead of looking at the big picture. While another team simply forgets about the list existed and continue their busywork business as usual.

**The data readiness is simply not there.** The use case looks clean and obvious, but the customer history is in a legacy tool, the numbers needed arrive sporadically by email on Tuesday and by Whatsapp on Friday, and some weeks not at all. The pilot dies on the get-go.

**The process itself is the problem.** Half of CEOs (50%) name AI technologies as the single largest contributor to productivity gains over the past 12 months, just ahead of business process redesign at 46%. ([EY-Parthenon CEO Outlook Survey](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2026/10/ai-drives-productivity-but-ceos-struggle-to-turn-gains-into-grow), October 2026) The two numbers are in close proximity, and the second one is the one companies skip. AI transformation is a fundamental change in how the work gets done. 

Point a model at a process that was never questioned and you get the same process, **maybe** running faster. **Sometimes the right decision is to re-design!**

## Making the first use case stick

The first use case is where the company either sees the AI value or gets disappointed. Four things to consider:
<ul class="success-list">
  <li><strong>Start small.</strong> One use case, one team, a few days to a week. Ship the small thing, learn, repeat.</li>
  <li><strong>Run it as an experiment.</strong> A hypothesis that can fail, a measure agreed before the start, and a date when you decide.</li>
  <li><strong>Set the goal in the language of the business.</strong> E.g. "The customer report goes out in two hours instead of two days".</li>
  <li><strong>Decide where the freed hours are spent.</strong> A pilot that works but changes nothing is a costly demo.</li>
</ul>

The fourth one is the one teams commonly skip. When the pilot works and the hours are saved, where are the hours spent? Left alone they fill up with the old way of working, because that is where the pressure already is.

Expect the emotional part, and give it room. The people closest to the work have the strongest view on whether a solution or a tool is any good, and they are knowledgeable about the details. They are also the ones who actually decide, whether the new way of doing sticks.

## Wrapping up

A pilot that ends without a solid decision teaches the company that AI was a waste of effort. Defining decision-making criteria upfront ensures you will be deciding about continuation promptly. That way you don't need to later fix cultural issues around AI use.
